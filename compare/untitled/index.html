The short answers

Questions artists actually ask.

Is Bridge.audio better than [untitled] for music creators?+

[untitled] is a strong fit for working on unfinished songs, exchanging versions and editing audio on the go. Bridge.audio is a stronger fit when you need professional catalog organization, metadata, AI-powered search, pitching and potential sync discovery. The two can also complement each other.

Can I keep working on demos in Bridge?+

You can store, organize and share work-in-progress audio in a Bridge Workspace. Bridge does not currently offer the same native recording, editing and version-history workflow as [untitled]. A full version-history feature for Bridge is not currently available.

Does Bridge help me reach labels and music supervisors?+

Bridge supports professional sharing and a label-directory workflow. Eligible rights holders can optionally publish music to Bridge Sync to be considered by relevant audiovisual music buyers. Visibility and opportunities are not guaranteed. A broader artist discovery Hub, Bridge Talents, has been publicly announced as in development, but is not available yet.

Which service is cheaper?+

Both offer a free starting option. The public Bridge Talent Pro plan starts at €5 per month with 250 tracks and five seats. The official [untitled] Membership is advertised at US$49.99 per year and includes unlimited storage. Since billing periods, limits and feature sets differ, there is no single fair cheapest-option answer.

Do I have to move away from [untitled] to use Bridge?+

No. Keep [untitled] for recording ideas, version collaboration and fans if those workflows suit you. Bridge can take over the professional side: organizing your music, sharing to teams, managing metadata and making eligible tracks discoverable through Bridge Sync.
